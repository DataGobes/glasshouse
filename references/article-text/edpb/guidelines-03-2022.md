# EDPB Guidelines 03/2022 on deceptive design patterns in social media platform interfaces: how to recognise and avoid them

**Source:** European Data Protection Board, Guidelines 03/2022 (Version 2.0, Final version).
**Retrieved:** 2026-05-11 from https://edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/guidelines/guidelines-032022-deceptive-design-patterns-social-media_en

**Adopted:** 14 March 2022 (Version 1.0, for public consultation); **Final version adopted:** 24 February 2023 (Version 2.0).

---

## Summary

These EDPB guidelines provide practical recommendations to designers and users of social media platforms on how to assess and avoid "deceptive design patterns" in interfaces that infringe the GDPR. They identify and categorise specific patterns — including overloading, skipping, stirring, obstructing, fickle and left in the dark — that may steer users into making choices that are not in their interest, such as giving consent that is not freely given, specific, informed or unambiguous within the meaning of Articles 4(11) and 7 GDPR, or that undermine the principles of fair and transparent processing (Article 5(1)(a) GDPR) and data protection by design and by default (Article 25 GDPR). The guidelines map each pattern to the relevant GDPR provisions and illustrate them with concrete UI examples spanning account creation, consent flows, settings, exiting accounts, exercising rights and information notices, providing best-practice recommendations to remedy each pattern.
